For an Indian Shopify merchant, this statement means that you, not Shopify, are legally responsible for the export transaction when selling internationally.

1. Merchant of Record (MoR)

As the Merchant of Record, your business is legally considered the seller.

You are responsible for:

Issuing invoices
Collecting payments
Export compliance
Customs declarations
GST compliance
Managing returns and disputes
Providing export documentation to authorities and banks

Example

If a customer in the UK buys a ₹1,500 tote bag from your Shopify store:

✅ The customer buys from your company

✅ Payment is received by your company

✅ The export is recorded under your IEC/GSTIN

✅ Bank remittance is credited to your account

2. Shipping Zones

You must configure which countries you ship to.

Example:

Zone	CountriesUK	United Kingdom
Europe	Germany, France, Italy, Spain
UAE	UAE
North America	USA, Canada

You also decide:

Shipping charges
Delivery timelines
Courier partners
Duties paid/unpaid model
3. HS Codes

HS (Harmonized System) Code is the customs classification for products.

Examples:

Product	Sample HS Code*Cotton Tote Bag	4202
Bedsheet	6302
Ceramic Plates	6912
Bathroom Accessories	Depends on material

* Exact code should be verified with your customs consultant or CHA.

Why it matters:

Customs duty calculation
Export declaration
Tax treatment
International shipping labels

Without HS codes, customs clearance can be delayed.

4. Export Remittance

When an overseas customer pays you:

Customer (UK)
      ↓
Shopify Store
      ↓
Payment Gateway
      ↓
Indian Bank Account


Indian export regulations require proof that foreign currency was received.

5. FIRC (Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate)

A bank-issued document showing:

Foreign payment received
Currency received
Sender details
Purpose of remittance

Used as proof of export earnings.

6. e-BRC (Electronic Bank Realisation Certificate)

An electronic certificate generated through the banking/export system confirming export proceeds were realized in India.

Benefits:

Export incentives (where applicable)
DGFT compliance
Export audits
Regulatory documentation
Practical Example

Suppose you sell:

Tote Bag = £12
Customer = London
Order on Shopify

You will:

Accept the order.
Generate export invoice.
Ship via DHL/FedEx/UPS.
Declare HS code.
Export under your IEC.
Receive payment in India.
Bank records foreign inward remittance.
Obtain FIRC/e-BRC if required.

Shopify does not do these compliance activities for Indian sellers.

What This Means for Your Planned UK/EU Tote Bag Business

As an Indian exporter selling through Shopify:

✅ You can sell globally from India.

✅ You do not need a UK company just to start selling via Shopify.

✅ You should have:

IEC (Import Export Code)
GST Registration
Current Account
International payment gateway (Shopify Payments is not available in India; typically Razorpay, PayPal, PayU, etc.)
Courier partner (DHL/FedEx/UPS/Aramex)

✅ Maintain export invoices and remittance records.

✅ Add correct HS codes to products.

✅ Keep FIRC/e-BRC records for compliance and banking purposes.

For most Indian Shopify exporters, the setup is essentially:

Indian Company
      +
IEC
      +
Shopify Store
      +
International Payment Gateway
      +
DHL/FedEx
      =
Global Export Business


This is actually the standard and preferred model for many Indian exporters selling tote bags, home décor, textiles, ceramics, and handicraft products directly to customers in the UK, EU, USA, and UAE.
